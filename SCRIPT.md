## Fact check (Hapgood translation, Project Gutenberg #135, Volume I Book 2)

- Arrives "early in the month of October, 1815, about an hour before sunset" (ch. I).
- "In October, 1815, he was released; he had entered there in 1796, for having broken a pane of glass and taken a loaf of bread" (ch. VI). His sister was a widow with seven children (ch. VI).
- Five years, then escape attempts added 3 + 5 + 3 + 3 years: four attempts, nineteen years (ch. VI).
- Yellow passport (ch. III, IX). Turned away by Jacquin Labarre's inn and the tavern on the Rue de Chaffaut, the prison ("The prison is not an inn"), and a dog's kennel where "the dog bit me and chased me off" (ch. I, III).
- The bishop calls him "monsieur"; Madame Magloire lights the two silver candlesticks and sets them on the table (ch. III).
- He takes the basket of silverware from the cupboard, throws the basket away, crosses the garden and leaps the wall (ch. XI). The empty basket is found in a flower bed (ch. XII).
- Gendarmes bring him back; "I gave you the candlesticks too ... Why did you not carry them away with your forks and spoons?" (ch. XII).
- "Jean Valjean, my brother, you no longer belong to evil, but to good. It is your soul that I buy from you ... and I give it to God." (ch. XII). Line 11 shortens this.
- Little Gervais, a Savoyard boy about ten, drops a forty-sou piece; "Jean Valjean set his foot upon it" (ch. XIII).
- At "a spot where three paths intersected", with the moon risen, he falls on a large stone: "It was the first time that he had wept in nineteen years" (ch. XIII).
