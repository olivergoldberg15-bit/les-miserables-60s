## Fact check (Hapgood translation, Project Gutenberg #135, Volume I)

- Arrives "early in the month of October, 1815, about an hour before sunset" (Book 2, ch. I).
- "He had entered there in 1796, for having broken a pane of glass and taken a loaf of bread"; his sister was a widow with seven children (Book 2, ch. VI).
- Five years, then four escape attempts added 3 + 5 + 3 + 3 years: nineteen (Book 2, ch. VI).
- Yellow passport (Book 2, ch. III, IX). Turned away by the inn of Jacquin Labarre and the tavern on the Rue de Chaffaut, the prison ("The prison is not an inn"), and a dog's kennel ("the dog bit me and chased me off") (Book 2, ch. I, III).
- The bishop gave the episcopal palace to the hospital and moved into the hospital's small house (Book 1, ch. II). "The door of our house is never fastened" (Book 1, ch. VI).
- He calls him "monsieur"; Madame Magloire lights the two silver candlesticks and sets them on the table (Book 2, ch. III).
- "As the Cathedral clock struck two in the morning, Jean Valjean awoke" (Book 2, ch. X).
- Near the bed, a ray of moonlight falls on the sleeping bishop's face; Valjean has the iron candlestick in his hand (Book 2, ch. XI).
- He takes the basket of silverware from the cupboard, throws the basket away, crosses the garden and leaps the wall (Book 2, ch. XI).
- Next morning, "The door opened. A singular and violent group made its appearance": three gendarmes and Jean Valjean (Book 2, ch. XII). The film cuts before the bishop's answer.
